Lesson 7.4 of Module 07 · Deep Comparative Philosophy

Advaita, Viśiṣṭādvaita & Dvaita — The Great Vedānta Debate

📖 Primary Source: Brahma Sutras (Swami Gambhirananda — 941 pp) · Chāndogya & Bṛhadāraṇyaka Upaniṣads  | 📚 Read in Library →

All Vedānta schools accept the Upanishads, Bhagavad Gītā, and Brahma Sūtras as absolute authority (*Prasthānatrayī*). However, they interpret the relationship between God (Brahman), the soul (Ātman), and the universe in 3 profound ways.

School 1 · Non-Dualism

1. Advaita Vedānta

Acharya: Ādi Śaṅkarācārya (8th Century CE)

Core View: Absolute Non-Dualism. Brahman alone is real; the world is an empirical appearance (*Māyā*); the soul (Ātman) IS non-different from Brahman.

Mahāvākya: "Aham Brahmāsmi" (I am Brahman).

School 2 · Qualified Non-Dualism

2. Viśiṣṭādvaita

Acharya: Śrī Rāmānujācārya (11th Century CE)

Core View: Qualified Non-Dualism. Souls and world are real, existing as the "body" of God (Nārāyaṇa). Like attributes to a substance, souls are distinct parts of One Supreme Lord.

Path: Loving Devotion (*Bhakti/Prapatti*).

School 3 · Dualism

3. Dvaita Vedānta

Acharya: Śrī Madhvācārya (13th Century CE)

Core View: Dualism. God (Viṣṇu), individual souls, and matter are eternally distinct and separate realities (*Panca-bheda*). The soul serves God in eternal devotion.

Path: Pure Grace & Service (*Sevā*).

🌊 The Wave & Ocean Analogy:

• Advaita: The wave IS the ocean. When the wave calms down, it realizes it was never separate from the ocean water.
• Viśiṣṭādvaita: The wave is a real part of the ocean. It cannot exist without the ocean, but it has its own distinct shape.
• Dvaita: The wave is the loving worshipper, and the ocean is the Almighty Lord. The wave finds eternal joy gazing upon the majestic ocean.
