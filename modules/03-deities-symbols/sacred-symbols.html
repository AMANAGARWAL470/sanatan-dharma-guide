Sacred Symbols of Sanātana Dharma — Decoded

📖 Primary Sources: Māṇḍūkya Upaniṣad · Śiva Purāṇa · Rig Veda Complete  | 📚 Read these books →

Every symbol in Sanātana Dharma carries a precise, deep meaning — they are not decorations. They are compressed encyclopaedias. Each one encodes an entire philosophical teaching in a single image.

ॐ
Symbol 1

AUM (OM) — The Primordial Sound

The Māṇḍūkya Upaniṣad devotes all 12 of its mantras to explaining the syllable AUM alone — it is that important.

ओमित्येतदक्षरमिदं सर्वं
तस्योपव्याख्यानं भूतं भवद्भविष्यदिति सर्वमोङ्कार एव
"AUM — this syllable is all this. All that is past, present, and future — all is indeed AUM. And whatever else there is beyond the three times — that too is AUM."
— Māṇḍūkya Upaniṣad, Mantra 1  |  Source: isakenakathapras0000upan.pdf — "Isa, Kena, Katha, Prasna, Mundaka, Mandukya Upanishads"
📚 Verify: Open Isa-Kena Upanishads in Library →
A (अ)

Waking State

Normal consciousness. The world you see with open eyes.

U (उ)

Dream State

The mind creates its own reality. Like a dream where you make the entire world.

M (म)

Deep Sleep

Pure rest. No thoughts, no dreams. Blissful unknowing.

Silence after AUM

Turīya — The Fourth

Pure Brahman. Pure awareness beyond all three states. This is your true nature.

卐
Symbol 2

Swastika (स्वस्तिक) — Good Fortune

Etymology: Su (सु) = well/good + Asti (अस्ति) = being/existence = "Well-being / Good Fortune." Used in Vedic culture for over 10,000 years before the Nazis misappropriated it in 1920 — rotated it 45°, and used it for opposite purposes.

स्वस्ति न इन्द्रो वृद्धश्रवाः
स्वस्ति नः पूषा विश्ववेदाः ।
स्वस्ति नस्तार्क्ष्यो अरिष्टनेमिः
स्वस्ति नो बृहस्पतिर्दधातु ॥
"May Indra of great fame grant us well-being (svasti). May Pūṣan, the all-knowing, grant us well-being. May Garuḍa, the destroyer of evil, grant us well-being. May Bṛhaspati grant us well-being."
— Rig Veda 1.89.6 (Svasti Mantra)  |  Source: Rig Veda Complete (Sakala Shakha)
📚 Verify: Open Rig Veda in Library →

The four arms of the Swastika represent the four Vedas, the four directions, the four stages of life (Āśramas), and the dynamic rotation of time/cosmos.

🪷
Symbol 3

Lotus (पद्म) — Purity in the World

The lotus grows out of muddy water, yet remains completely clean — not a drop of mud clings to its petals. This is the central metaphor of Karma Yoga:

ब्रह्मण्याधाय कर्माणि सङ्गं त्यक्त्वा करोति यः ।
लिप्यते न स पापेन पद्मपत्रमिवाम्भसा ॥
"One who performs his duty without attachment, surrendering the results to God, is not affected by sinful action — just as a lotus leaf is never wetted by water."
— Bhagavad Gītā 5.10  |  Source: Gita Sadhak Sanjivani, Chapter 5, pg. 310

Symbol 4

Triśūla (Trident) — Śiva's Three Powers

The three prongs of Śiva's Triśūla represent three fundamental realities that must be transcended for liberation:

Prong 1

Icchā Śakti

Will power / desire force. The impulse to create and act.

Prong 2

Jñāna Śakti

Knowledge power. The force of wisdom and awareness.

Prong 3

Kriyā Śakti

Action power. The force of physical manifestation.
