Śānti Parva — Bhīṣma's Deathbed Masterclass

📖 Primary Source: Mahābhārata, Śānti Parva (Parva 12 — 14,000 Verses in 3 Ahnika Sections)  | 📚 Read in Library →

Śānti Parva is the largest Parva in the Mahābhārata (14,000 verses). Following the tragic war, King Yudhiṣṭhira is overwhelmed by grief and remorse. Śrī Kṛṣṇa leads the Pāṇḍavas to Bhīṣmācārya, who is lying on a bed of arrows awaiting the auspicious moment of death (*Uttarāyaṇa*). Kṛṣṇa asks Bhīṣma — the ultimate authority on Dharma — to instruct Yudhiṣṭhira on statecraft, ethics, and liberation.

Section 1

1. Rājadharma Parva

Duties of Kings & Government: Taxation rules (taxes must be collected like a bee collects nectar without harming the flower), justice system, army, and welfare.

Section 2

2. Āpaddharma Parva

Ethics in Times of Crisis: How moral rules adapt when society or an individual is under extreme existential threat, preserving survival without losing moral core.

Section 3

3. Mokṣadharma Parva

Path to Spiritual Freedom: Deep philosophical discourses on Samkhya, Yoga, Vedanata, detachment, meditation, and overcoming grief.

प्रजासुखे सुखं राज्ञः प्रजानां च हिते हितम् ।
नात्मप्रियं हितं राज्ञः प्रजानां तु प्रियं हितम् ॥
"In the happiness of his subjects lies the king's happiness; in their welfare his welfare. Whatever pleases himself is not considered good by the ruler, but whatever pleases his subjects is good for him."
— Mahābhārata, Śānti Parva (also cited in Arthaśāstra 1.19.34)
