The 5 Core Principles of Sanātana Dharma

📖 Primary Sources: 108 Upanishads · Brihadāraṇyaka Upaniṣad · Katha Upaniṣad  |  📚 Read these books →

All of Sanātana Dharma rests on five foundational concepts. Once you truly understand these five, everything else — the many Gods, the rituals, the yoga, the festivals — will make complete sense. Let's go through each one with real analogies and the actual shlokas (verses) from the Upanishads.

1. Brahman (ब्रह्मन्) — The One Ultimate Reality

Brahman is not a person or a God you pray to. Brahman is the infinite, eternal, all-pervading Reality that is the source and substance of everything that exists.

सर्वं खल्विदं ब्रह्म
तज्जलानिति शान्त उपासीत
"All this universe is indeed Brahman. Everything is born from Brahman, dissolves into Brahman, and breathes in Brahman. Knowing this — meditate in peace."
— Chāndogya Upaniṣad 3.14.1  |  Source: 108 Upanishads (108upanishads.pdf), pg. 312
📚 Verify: Open 108 Upanishads in Library →
🪙 Real-Life Analogy

Think of gold and gold ornaments. A ring, a bangle, a chain, a crown — all look different, all have different names and functions. But they are all just gold. Brahman is like the gold. All the different forms you see — mountains, oceans, animals, humans, stars — are the ornaments. The underlying substance is always one.

2. Ātman (आत्मन्) — The Eternal Self Within You

Ātman is the immortal soul that lives inside every living being. Not your body, not your thoughts, not your emotions — but the pure, unchanging awareness that witnesses all of those. And here is the most radical teaching:

अयम् आत्मा ब्रह्म
"This Ātman (individual soul) IS Brahman (the Ultimate Reality)."
— Māṇḍūkya Upaniṣad, Mantra 2  |  Source: 108 Upanishads (108upanishads.pdf), pg. 451
This is one of the 4 Mahāvākyas (Great Sayings) — the most important statements in all the Upanishads.
📚 Verify: Open 108 Upanishads in Library →
🌊 Real-Life Analogy

A wave on the ocean looks separate and individual. It has its own shape, its own height, its own movement. But the wave is the ocean. It's made of ocean, it came from the ocean, and it will return to the ocean. Your individual soul (Ātman) is like the wave. The Ocean is Brahman. Separation is just an appearance.

3. Karma (कर्म) — The Law of Action & Its Fruit

Karma is not fate or luck. It is the cosmic law that every action produces a corresponding result. Not just physical actions — thoughts and words create karma too.

यज्ञार्थात्कर्मणोऽन्यत्र लोकोऽयं कर्मबन्धनः
"Work done as an offering (yajna — sacred action) does not bind you. All other action, done for selfish ends, binds the doer to its results."
— Bhagavad Gītā 3.9  |  Source: Gita Sadhak Sanjivani (Gita-Sadhak-Sanjevani-English-old.pdf), pg. 198
📚 Verify: Open Gita Sadhak Sanjivani in Library →
🌱 Real-Life Analogy

Karma is like planting seeds. Plant mango seeds → you will get mangoes. Plant thorns → you get thorns. You don't always see the result immediately — just like a seed takes time to grow into a tree — but the result always comes. This is why Sanātana Dharma says: be careful what you think, say, and do.

4. Māyā (माया) — The Illusion of Separation

Māyā is what makes us feel like separate, isolated individuals who are born, live, and die. It is the cosmic "veil" that hides our true nature — that we are one with Brahman.

यस्तु सर्वाणि भूतान्यात्मन्येवानुपश्यति
सर्वभूतेषु चात्मानं ततो न विजुगुप्सते
"He who sees all beings in his own Self, and his own Self in all beings — he does not hate or fear anything."
— Īśā Upaniṣad, Verse 6  |  Source: isakenakathapras0000upan.pdf, pg. 8
📚 Verify: Open Isa-Kena Upanishads in Library →
🎬 Real-Life Analogy

Māyā is like being absorbed in a movie. You're sitting safely in a cinema hall, but you're so caught up in the film that you feel fear, sorrow, and excitement as if it were real. The moment someone taps you on the shoulder, you remember: "Oh! I'm in a cinema. This is a film." Spiritual practice is that tap on the shoulder — waking you up from the film of "I am just this body, and I am separate from everything."

5. Mokṣa (मोक्ष) — Liberation

Mokṣa is the ultimate goal — liberation from the cycle of birth, death, and rebirth (saṃsāra). But it doesn't mean you disappear — it means you wake up to what you truly are.

नायमात्मा बलहीनेन लभ्यः
न च प्रमादात् तपसो वाप्यलिङ्गात्
"This Ātman cannot be attained by the weak — nor by the careless, nor by the one without spiritual practice. Only through vigorous effort, right living, and sincere seeking does one reach it."
— Muṇḍaka Upaniṣad 3.2.4  |  Source: Katha-Upanishad.pdf, pg. 18
📚 Verify: Open Katha Upanishad in Library →
😴 Real-Life Analogy

Imagine you're having a nightmare — you're running from something terrifying. Mokṣa is like waking up from the nightmare. You're not dead — you're more awake than before. The fear, the running, the suffering — all gone. What remains is calm awareness. That is mokṣa: the recognition that you were always free, always the Ātman, and the suffering was only in the dream.

Quick Reference: The 5 Principles

Core Truth 1

Brahman

The one infinite Reality. Everything that exists is Brahman.

Core Truth 2

Ātman

Your true self — the eternal soul. Same as Brahman in essence.

Core Truth 3

Karma

Every action has a consequence. Plant good seeds.

Core Truth 4

Māyā

The illusion of separation that we need to wake up from.

Core Truth 5

Mokṣa

Liberation — recognising your true nature as pure, eternal awareness. This is the goal of Sanātana Dharma.
