Lesson 9.2 of Module 09 · Phonetics & Sacred Sound

Sanskrit Pronunciation & Vedic Chanting Rules

📖 Primary Source: Pāṇini Śikṣā · Taittirīya Prātiśākhya · Rig Veda Sakala Shakha  | 📚 Read in Library →

Sanskrit is a phonetically perfect language. Every sound (*Varna*) originates from a precise location in the vocal tract. Proper pronunciation ensures that the vibrational potency (*Spandana*) of Vedic mantras is preserved.

The 5 Vocal Organs (Stāna)

Origin 1 · Throat

Kanthya (Gutturals)

Sounds created in the back of the throat: a, ā, k, kh, g, gh, ṅ, h.

Origin 2 · Hard Palate

Tālavya (Palatals)

Tongue presses flat against roof of mouth: i, ī, c, ch, j, jh, ñ, ś, y.

Origin 3 · Roof of Mouth

Mūrdhanya (Retroflex)

Tip of tongue curls back to touch roof: ṛ, ṝ, ṭ, ṭh, ḍ, ḍh, ṇ, ṣ, r.

Origin 4 · Teeth

Dantya (Dentals)

Tip of tongue touches behind upper teeth: t, th, d, dh, n, s, l.

Origin 5 · Lips

Oṣṭhya (Labials)

Sounds formed purely by bringing lips together: u, ū, p, ph, b, bh, m.

Vedic Accents

Svara (Pitches)

Udātta (high pitch), Anudātta (low pitch), Svarita (falling tone).
