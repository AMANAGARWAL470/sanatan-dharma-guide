Daily Habits, Yoga & Meditation

📖 Primary Sources: Raja Yoga — Swami Vivekananda (230 pp) · Patañjali's Yoga Sūtras  | 📚 Read Raja Yoga →

Sanātana Dharma is not just theory — it is deeply practical. The tradition has a complete science of daily living that takes you from waking up in the morning to going to sleep at night, embedding spiritual awareness into every action.

What is Yoga? — The Real Definition

Most people think Yoga = physical stretching. The original definition, from Patañjali's Yoga Sūtras (the foundational text of Yoga philosophy), is something completely different:

योगश्चित्तवृत्तिनिरोधः
"Yoga is the cessation (nirodha) of the fluctuations (vṛtti) of the mind-field (citta)."
— Patañjali Yoga Sūtras 1.2  |  Explained in Raja-Yoga-by-Swami-Vivekananda.pdf, Chapter 1, pg. 15
📚 Verify: Open Raja Yoga by Vivekananda in Library →
🌊 What does this mean?

Imagine your mind is an ocean. Thoughts are waves — constantly moving, distorting the surface. When there are no waves, you can see straight to the bottom — clearly, perfectly. Yoga is the practice of calming those waves. When the mind is completely calm — that calm, clear awareness is your true nature (Ātman). That is the state of Yoga.

The 8 Limbs of Yoga — Aṣṭāṅga Yoga

Patañjali gives a complete, systematic 8-step path to this mental calm. Physical postures (Āsana) are only Step 3:

Limb 1

Yama — Social Ethics

Ahiṃsā (non-violence), Satya (truth), Asteya (non-stealing), Brahmacarya (right use of energy), Aparigraha (non-possessiveness).

📖 Raja Yoga, Chapter 2 — Swami Vivekananda
Limb 2

Niyama — Personal Discipline

Śauca (purity), Santoṣa (contentment), Tapas (self-discipline), Svādhyāya (self-study), Īśvara-praṇidhāna (surrender to God).

📖 Raja Yoga, Chapter 2
Limb 3

Āsana — Posture

A stable, comfortable seated position for meditation. NOT a gym workout — the purpose is to make the body still enough that the mind can go inward.

Limb 4

Prāṇāyāma — Breath Regulation

Control of the vital life-force (Prāṇa) through conscious breath regulation. When the breath is calm, the mind calms automatically.

Limb 5

Pratyāhāra — Withdrawal

Withdrawing the senses inward — like a tortoise drawing its limbs into its shell. Stopping the constant intake of external stimulation.

Limbs 6–8

Dhāraṇā → Dhyāna → Samādhi

Dhāraṇā = Concentration (fixing the mind on one point). Dhyāna = Unbroken meditation. Samādhi = Complete absorption — the meditator, the act of meditation, and the object of meditation become one.

📖 Raja Yoga, Chapters 3–4

Swami Vivekananda on Meditation

[From Raja Yoga, Chapter 4]
"Meditation is the means of unification of the subject and the object. In meditation there is no longer the feeling of the ego, the little self. The meditator and the meditated upon become one... This is the superconscious state called Samādhi."
— Raja Yoga by Swami Vivekananda, Chapter 4  |  Source: Raja-Yoga-by-Swami-Vivekananda.pdf, pg. 167

Simple Daily Practice for a Beginner

Morning

5-Minute Practice

Sit quietly. Take 10 slow, deep breaths. Chant AUM 3 times. Offer a small prayer of gratitude before starting the day.

Action

Ahiṃsā in Daily Life

Do not harm — in thought, word, or action. This single practice transforms all your relationships.

Evening

Reflection

Before sleep: "Did I act from fear/ego today — or from truth/dharma?" No judgment — just honest awareness.

Weekly

Study

Read a few verses of the Gita or Upanishads. Even 5 minutes of reading from the primary source books changes the mind's quality.
