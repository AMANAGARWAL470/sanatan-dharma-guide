The Upanishads — The Science of the Self

📖 Primary Sources: 108 Upanishads · Brihadāraṇyaka Upaniṣad · Katha Upaniṣad · Isa-Kena Upanishads  | 📚 Read these books →

If the Vedas are the roots of Sanātana Dharma, the Upanishads are the flowers — the highest philosophical teachings ever committed to writing. "Upanishad" means "sitting close to the teacher" — these are intimate one-on-one dialogues between masters and students on the deepest questions of existence.

What is the Ultimate Goal of the Upanishads?

तमेव विदित्वाऽतिमृत्युमेति
नान्यः पन्था विद्यतेऽयनाय
"By knowing Him alone, one transcends death. There is no other path for liberation."
— Śvetāśvatara Upaniṣad 3.8  |  Source: 108upanishads.pdf

The 4 Mahāvākyas — The Greatest Sentences Ever Spoken

The Mahāvākyas (Great Sayings) are four statements from four different Upanishads that encapsulate the entire teaching of Vedānta in one line each. These are considered the most powerful sentences in all of Indian philosophy:

Mahāvākya 1 — from Aitareya Upanishad
प्रज्ञानं ब्रह्म
"Pure Consciousness is Brahman."
— Aitareya Upaniṣad 3.3 (Rig Veda branch)
📚 Verify →
Mahāvākya 2 — from Brihadāraṇyaka Upanishad
अहं ब्रह्मास्मि
"I am Brahman." — The individual soul is identical to Ultimate Reality.
— Brihadāraṇyaka Upaniṣad 1.4.10  |  Brihadaranyaka-Upanishad.pdf
📚 Verify →
Mahāvākya 3 — from Chāndogya Upanishad
तत् त्वम् असि
"That (Brahman) thou art." — You are not separate from the ultimate Reality.
— Chāndogya Upaniṣad 6.8.7  |  108upanishads.pdf
📚 Verify →
Mahāvākya 4 — from Māṇḍūkya Upanishad
अयम् आत्मा ब्रह्म
"This Ātman (Self) is Brahman." — Your inner self is the infinite universe.
— Māṇḍūkya Upaniṣad 2  |  108upanishads.pdf, pg. 451
📚 Verify →
💡 What do these 4 Mahāvākyas mean together?

They all say the same thing from four different angles: The individual soul (you) and the universal Reality (God/Brahman) are not two different things — they are One. The feeling of separation is due to Māyā (illusion). When you fully understand this — that is Mokṣa.

The Story of Nachiketas — From the Katha Upanishad

The Katha Upanishad contains one of the greatest stories in all spiritual literature — a young boy named Nachiketas who travels to the realm of Yama (Death) and refuses three tempting worldly gifts in order to receive the ultimate knowledge of the immortal Self:

न जायते म्रियते वा विपश्चित्
नायं कुतश्चिन्न बभूव कश्चित् ।
अजो नित्यः शाश्वतोऽयं पुराणो
न हन्यते हन्यमाने शरीरे ॥
"The wise (Self) is not born, nor does it die. It did not come from anywhere, nor did anything come from it. It is unborn, eternal, everlasting, and primeval. It is not slain when the body is slain."
— Katha Upaniṣad 1.2.18  |  Source: Katha-Upanishad.pdf, pg. 9 (Also quoted by Krishna in Bhagavad Gita 2.20)
