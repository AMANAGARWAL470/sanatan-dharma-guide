What is Sanātana Dharma?

Simple Definition

Sanātana = Timeless / Eternal (Without beginning or end).
Dharma = Right action, moral order, or natural law (like gravity or the sun giving light).

Together, Sanātana Dharma means "The Timeless Natural Order of Life". It is not an invention of a single person; it is the study of how life and the universe work.

Religion vs. Dharma — What's the Difference?

In Western languages, Sanātana Dharma is usually translated as "Hinduism". But there is a big difference between a traditional dogma and a Dharma:

Organized Religion

  • Founded by a single historical human prophet.
  • Centerminded around one mandatory book.
  • Demands belief in a set creed ("Believe or be condemned").

Sanātana Dharma

  • No single human founder or start date.
  • A vast library of books for different personalities.
  • Encourages questioning and self-discovery (*"Seek and realize truth yourself"*).

"Ekam Sat Vipra Bahudha Vadanti"

Translation: Truth is One; the wise describe It in many ways. (Rigveda 1.164.46)
