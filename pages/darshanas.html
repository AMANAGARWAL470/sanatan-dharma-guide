The 6 Schools of Philosophy षड्-दर्शन

In Sanātana Dharma, philosophy is called Darśana (direct vision of Truth). The six orthodox systems accept Vedic authority while providing distinct, complementary perspectives on physics, logic, psychology, metaphysics, and liberation.

Founder: Sage Kapila · Text: Sāṃkhyakārikā

1. Sāṃkhya Darśana सांख्य दर्शन

Analyzed in Sāṃkhyakārikā (206 pages in Sinha edition). The oldest systematic philosophy in India. Enumerates 25 principles of reality divided into:

  • Puruṣa: Pure Unchanging Consciousness. Infinite, eternal, and distinct from matter.
  • Prakṛti: Primordial Creative Nature composed of 3 Gunas (Sattva, Rajas, Tamas).
  • Evolutes of Prakṛti (23 Tattvas): Intellect (Mahat/Buddhi), Ego (Ahaṁkāra), Mind (Manas), 5 Senses, 5 Action Organs, 5 Subtle Elements (Tanmātras), and 5 Gross Elements (Mahābhūtas).
Founder: Sage Patañjali · Commentary: Swami Vivekananda's Rāja Yoga

2. Yoga Darśana योग दर्शन

Based on Patanjali's 196 Yoga Sūtras. Builds upon Sāṃkhya metaphysics by adding practical mental technology to quiet the mind and attain Kaivalya (Liberation).

योगश्चित्तवृत्तिनिरोधः ॥ (Yoga Sūtra 1.2)
"Yoga is the complete stillness of the modifications of the mind-stuff (Citta)."

The Eight-Fold Path (Aṣṭāṅga Yoga)

Step 1

Yama

5 Moral Restraints: Ahiṃsā, Satya, Asteya, Brahmacarya, Aparigraha.

Step 2

Niyama

5 Observances: Śauca, Saṁtoṣa, Tapas, Svādhyāya, Īśvarapraṇidhāna.

Step 3

Āsana

Steady and comfortable physical posture for meditation.

Step 4

Prāṇāyāma

Regulation and mastery of vital breath energy.

Step 5

Pratyāhāra

Withdrawal of senses from external objects.

Step 6

Dhāraṇā

One-pointed concentration of mind on a single focus.

Step 7

Dhyāna

Unbroken stream of meditation/contemplation.

Step 8

Samādhi

Complete absorption in pure Consciousness.

Founder: Sage Kaṇāda · Text: Vaiśeṣika Sūtras

3. Vaiśeṣika Darśana वैशेषिक दर्शन

Extracted from Kaṇāda's Vaiśeṣika Sūtras (380 pages in Nandalal Sinha edition). Developed ancient atomic theory (Paramāṇuvāda), asserting that all physical matter is composed of indivisible, indestructible atoms governed by unseen moral laws (Adṛṣṭa).

Founder: Sage Gautama · Text: Nyāya Sūtras

4. Nyāya Darśana न्याय दर्शन

School of rigorous logic and epistemology. Establishes the 4 valid means of acquiring true knowledge (Pramāṇa):

  • Pratyakṣa: Direct sensory perception.
  • Anumāna: Logical inference (famous 5-step syllogism).
  • Upamāna: Analogy and comparison.
  • Śabda: Verbal testimony of trustworthy seers.
Founder: Sage Jaimini

5. Mīmāṃsā (Pūrva-Mīmāṃsā)

Focuses on ritual exegesis, duty (Dharma), and the exact science of sound vibrations and Vedic commands.

Founder: Bādarāyaṇa · Bhāṣya: Ādi Śaṅkara

6. Vedānta (Uttara-Mīmāṃsā)

Based on 941 pages of Brahma Sūtra Bhāṣya. Inquiry into Brahman. Includes Advaita (Non-duality), Viśiṣṭādvaita, and Dvaita.
