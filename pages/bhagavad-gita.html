Bhagavad Gītā — 18 Chapters Explored श्रीमद्भगवद्गीता

Based on Sādhaka Sañjīvanī (2,159 pages by Swami Ramsukhdas) and classical commentaries. The 18 chapters are divided into 3 six-chapter sections (*Ṣaṭkas*): Karma Yoga (Ch 1–6), Bhakti Yoga (Ch 7–12), and Jñāna Yoga (Ch 13–18).

Part 1: Karma Yoga & Self-Mastery (Chapters 1 – 6)

Chapter 01 · 47 Verses

Arjuna Viṣāda Yoga — The Despondency of Arjuna

On the battlefield of Kurukṣetra, Arjuna is overwhelmed by grief and moral dilemma seeing relatives arrayed for war. He drops his bow Gandiva, refusing to fight.

सीदन्ति मम गात्राणि मुखं च परिशुष्यति । वेपथुश्च शरीरे मे रोमहर्षश्च जायते ॥ (1.29)
"My limbs fail and my mouth is parched; my body quivers and my hair stands on end!"
Chapter 02 · 72 Verses · Core Summary

Sāṃkhya Yoga — The Yoga of Knowledge & Sthitaprajña

Lord Kṛṣṇa begins His divine teaching by revealing the immortal nature of Ātman (which weapons cannot cleave nor fire burn) and introduces Karma Yoga and the qualities of the steady-minded sage (*Sthitaprajña*).

न जायते म्रियते वा कदाचिन्नायं भूत्वा भविता वा न भूयः । अजो नित्यः शाश्वतोऽयं पुराणो न हन्यते हन्यमाने शरीरे ॥ (2.20)
na jāyate mriyate vā kadācin nāyaṁ bhūtvā bhavitā vā na bhūyaḥ | ajo nityaḥ śāśvato'yaṁ purāṇo na hanyate hanyamāne śarīre
"The soul is never born, nor does it ever die; nor having once been, does it cease to be. It is unborn, eternal, ever-existing, and primeval. It is not slain when the body is slain."
Chapter 03 · 43 Verses

Karma Yoga — The Path of Action

Teaches that no one can remain actionless for even a moment. Action must be performed as sacrifice (*Yajña*) for the welfare of the world (*Lokasaṁgraha*).

Chapter 04 · 42 Verses

Jñāna Karma Sannyāsa Yoga — Wisdom & Incarnation

Reveals the secret of Divine Incarnation (*Avatāra*) to protect Dharma, and how knowledge burns all past Karmas into ashes like a blazing fire.

यदा यदा हि धर्मस्य ग्लानिर्भवति भारत । अभ्युत्थानमधर्मस्य तदात्मानं सृजाम्यहम् ॥ (4.7)
"Whenever there is a decline of righteousness and a rise of unrighteousness, O Arjuna, then I manifest Myself."
Chapter 05 · 29 Verses

Karma Sannyāsa Yoga — Renunciation of Action

Reconciles exterior renunciation with internal detachment, proving that true renunciation is performing duty without ego or attachment.

Chapter 06 · 47 Verses

Dhyāna Yoga (Ātmasaṁyama) — Meditation & Mind Control

Detailed instructions on meditation seat, posture, breath control, and mastering the restless mind through practice (*Abhyāsa*) and detachment (*Vairāgya*).

उद्धरेदात्मनात्मानं नात्मानमवसादयेत् । आत्मैव ह्यात्मनो बन्धुरात्मैव रिपुरात्मनः ॥ (6.5)
"Elevate yourself by your own mind; do not degrade yourself. For the mind alone is the friend of the self, and the mind alone is the enemy of the self."

Part 2: Bhakti Yoga & Cosmic Manifestation (Chapters 7 – 12)

Chapter 07

Jñāna Vijñāna Yoga

Distinction between lower physical nature (Aparā Prakṛti) and higher spiritual nature (Parā Prakṛti). 4 types of devotees (distressed, seeker of wealth, seeker of knowledge, and the wise).

Chapter 08

Akṣara Brahma Yoga

The science of departure at death (*Antakāla*). What a person remembers at the final moment of life determines their next destination.

Chapter 09

Rājavidyā Rājaguhya Yoga

The sovereign secret knowledge. God sustains all creation while remaining unattached, accepting even a leaf, flower, fruit, or water offered with love (*Patraṁ Puṣpaṁ Phalaṁ Toyam*).

Chapter 10

Vibhūti Yoga

Divine Opulences. Lord Kṛṣṇa lists His prominent manifestations: *"Among seers I am Vyāsa, among mountains I am Himālaya, among letters I am AUM."*

Chapter 11

Viśvarūpa Darśana Yoga

The Cosmic Form. Kṛṣṇa grants Arjuna divine vision to behold the infinite universe, suns, gods, and time operating within His single cosmic body.

Chapter 12

Bhakti Yoga

The Path of Pure Devotion. Outlines the 39 divine qualities of a true devotee—free from malice, forgiving, balanced in pleasure and pain.

Part 3: Jñāna Yoga & Ultimate Freedom (Chapters 13 – 18)

Chapter 13

Kṣetra Kṣetrajña Yoga

Distinction between the Field (the body/mind) and the Knower of the Field (the conscious Soul).

Chapter 14

Guṇatraya Vibhāga Yoga

The 3 Gunas: Sattva (Purity/Light), Rajas (Passion/Activity), and Tamas (Inertia/Ignorance).

Chapter 15

Puruṣottama Yoga

The Cosmic Tree (Aśvattha) with roots above and branches below. Supreme Person beyond manifest and unmanifest.

Chapter 16

Daivāsura Sampad Yoga

Divine vs. Demonic qualities. Lists 26 divine virtues (Fearlessness, Purity, Charity) and 3 gates to hell (Lust, Anger, Greed).

Chapter 17

Śraddhātraya Vibhāga Yoga

Threefold faith, food, sacrifice, austerity, and charity according to the 3 Gunas. Uses Om Tat Sat.

Chapter 18

Mokṣa Sannyāsa Yoga

The climax of Gita wisdom. Final surrender (*Charama Śloka* 18.66): *"Abandon all forms of egoic duties and surrender unto Me alone; I shall liberate you."*
